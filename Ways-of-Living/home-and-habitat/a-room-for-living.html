A Room for Living

Making room for everyday life

The room matters through what it makes possible: a meal prepared together, work near the window, a book within reach, a little floor left open.

I grew up around building and renovation. My parents built two houses and renovated homes and retail spaces. I designed and built a home of my own, and have taken part in making and remaking places throughout my life. Years of travel have brought me into many different rooms. Some are arranged so life settles in easily. Others leave you wondering where your things—or the day itself—can go.

A studio or one-bedroom rental makes the choices visible. Two people may share the kitchen, the table, the light, and the paths through the room. It is a useful beginning for thinking about a home of any size. The particular arrangement grows from the people, the place, and the life around it.

Window Place

A window brings daylight into the room and gives the eyes somewhere to go. At a desk or kitchen table, there is a view to return to between moments at the screen. Light can fall across the work surface and the person sitting there. In a video call, that natural light is useful too.

Color can enter the frame in small ways: a green plant, a blue object, a red shirt or scarf, a piece of art. I find that having red, green, and blue in the scene helps the camera pick up color. These touches can belong to the room or to the person using it.

A Kitchen Ready for Living

Can two people prepare something to eat without moving everything around? Are the tools that get used close at hand? A few good basics and a clear work surface can make a small kitchen feel ready for living.

Kitchen Essentials for Two

  • Cookware: stockpot, saucepan, larger and smaller frying pans
  • Dishes and tableware: four dinner plates, four side plates, four bowls, four sets of cutlery, mugs, and drinking glasses
  • Preparation and serving: sharp kitchen knife, cutting board, serving bowl, water pitcher, whisk, ladle, turner, flexible silicone scraper, and serving spoon
  • Useful additions: colander, can opener, peeler, and baking tray

I prefer stainless steel or cast iron cookware. I also travel with small jars of the spices I like to use.

Things from Your Life

A room holds the things people bring to it: clothes, books, work materials, art, familiar objects. A bookshelf can hold both books and kitchenware. Everyday dishes can stay visible and easy to reach, while open shelves leave room for the people who will use them.

A room can hold the things that matter and still leave space for life to unfold.

Room to Move

A clear patch of floor gives the room another kind of usefulness. A yoga mat offers one familiar measure; I like about a foot of open floor around it when the room allows. That space can hold stretching, sitting, play, or dancing. It can also simply remain open.

Furniture, storage, light, and belongings form the setting. The people who live there bring it to life.

A Place Reading brings this attention to a particular home or rental: how the rooms are used, what already works, and what changes could make daily life easier and more pleasurable. The work begins with the place and the people it is meant to hold.
