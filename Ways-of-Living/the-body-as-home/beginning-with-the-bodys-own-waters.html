Beginning withthe Body’s Own Waters

How my Shivambu practice began.

BEGINNING

My relationship with Shivambu began with a person, not a book. I was watching an interview with Dave Murphy, known online as Allegedly Dave. He spoke about his experience, and I was instantly intrigued. It moved me enough that I didn’t feel I needed to read anything more. I took that self-responsibility into my own hands and went for it. I started drinking that same day, and I never stopped.

I never Googled it. There was already that old feeling that this was a dirty thing, but I never went looking and never got flooded with what so many people meet first. Direct experience was going to give me what I needed, and I’m really glad I began that way.

The timing mattered. We had just moved, and I had done a lot of heavy lifting. I have had radial shortening in both arms, because I tore the blood vessels off the lunate bone in my wrist, and I’ve lived with intense wrist pain for most of my adult life. After the move, my wrists were really inflamed and sore.

I began with a three-day fast, looping all of my Shivambu. By the second day, I was astonished at how much better my wrists felt. Mobility came back. The pain dissipated. It wasn’t perfect, and it never will be, but it was remarkable how much better I felt, and my wrists have never gone back to the way they were.

That was the first thing I noticed. That fast also changed how I understood fasting, and Shivambu became a spiritual practice for me. It took away a lot of fear. There is a huge emotional and spiritual part to this practice; it has even touched my dreaming. I think that’s one reason so many people who practise don’t speak about it publicly. It is very sacred.

There have been many other experiences with my body since, but that was what started me. Only then did I begin to read. The first books were John Armstrong’s The Water of Life and Martha Christy’s Your Own Perfect Medicine, and they took me into a deep dive of reading and research that continues to this day.

FIRST STEPS

No Right or Wrong Way

I always tell people there’s no right or wrong way to start. Everyone begins at their own comfort level. Sometimes that’s a few days of just capturing it in a glass jar and observing it. Sometimes it’s a drop on the hand, or a drop under the tongue, or a glassful. And sometimes people like me do a fast on it. It’s a self-guided practice.

What you put into your body, you taste. Some people choose a day of lighter or cleaner eating before they begin. Others, like me, just start, and things change. The more you practise, the more you see the impact of food, and it becomes an awareness. You start to eat better because you can taste the difference.

THE NAME

The Name

Shivambu comes from the Sanskrit śivāmbu: śiva and ambu, water. So it is commonly rendered “water of Shiva,” a spiritual language for one’s own urine. The words we use change how we approach something: “urine,” “Shivambu,” and “the body’s own waters” can stir very different feelings, even though they name the same thing.

THE WATERS

The Waters We Began In

The kidneys are always at work on balance, regulating water, salts, and what is dissolved in us; urine is that work made visible. And before we were born, we lived in our own waters. Later in pregnancy, the baby’s own urine becomes a major part of the amniotic fluid, and the baby swallows it continually, so the fluid circulates, cushioning, supporting movement, helping the lungs to grow.

We don’t have to drain the wonder from physiology to explain it accurately.

PRIVACY

A Personal Journey

For many people, the hardest part isn’t the practice. It’s the people they live with. Partners and families aren’t always ready to hear about it, and there can be a lot of pushback. I tell people that women don’t usually talk to their partners and children about how they’re handling their moon cycle, their menstruation, and this sits in a similar place. It’s personal. You can begin privately, and share when you’re ready, with boundaries and with your feet on the ground.

THE MOON CYCLE

The Moon Cycle

I continue through my moon cycle. How I meet it changes with how I’m feeling, what’s going on in my life, and where I am, and it has changed with the seasons of my own life too. That part I share in the workshop and the circle.

ON THE MOVE

On the Move

Being nomadic adds its own twist. When I’m in transit, I always carry an empty water bottle, and I always at least capture it. I call it the sip cycle: I take a sip of every cycle, and if I want to drink all of it, I do. Otherwise I discard it, or keep it in the bottle so I have the option, and refill it next time. Aged Shivambu needs a month, so I only have it when I stay somewhere long enough, or where I’ve left a stash. The practice adapts to the life.

STILL

Still Captivated

I’ve practised every day since 3 April 2022, and I’m still completely captivated by it, and I always will be. It is part of how I live now. I’m pretty much always in contact with it in some way, and there will not be a day when I don’t have contact with these waters. I’ve dedicated my life to researching them.

Shivambu: Beginning is where I share that beginning with others. You can come with a long-held question, a story of your own, or simply a wish to listen.
