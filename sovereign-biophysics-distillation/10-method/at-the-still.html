Method

At the Still

Equipment

The early runs used a manually operated 4-litre VEVOR YMK40202. The current still is a 4-litre VEVOR YMK40207 with a timer and adjustable temperature.

Starting material

The records distinguish fresh urine from aged urine. For aged material, relevant details include how long it was stored, whether the container was open or sealed, and what was known about diet when it was collected.

The urine varies in clarity and in how much it foams. Clearer samples have been observed during periods of simpler meals. Larger batches have sometimes been possible with that material.

Current batches are 500 mL or less, and most begin with about 300 mL. The smaller volume leaves room for foam to rise in the chamber and limits carryover into the distillate.

Time and temperature

The early still was timed by hand and switched off before its protective cutoff. With the newer still, repeated runs of about 300 mL established a working run time. The built-in timer now ends heating at the planned point, with liquid still in the chamber. The still is attended and the amount left is checked.

At altitude, reduced air pressure lowers the boiling point, and the temperature setting is adjusted accordingly. Changes in run behaviour are also observed as high- and low-pressure weather systems pass.

What is collected

In the opening distillation comparison, the liquid that condenses is collected together as the whole distillate. Some records call this pure plasma.

In later runs, fractions are collected separately. The early fraction is the spirit, and the middle fraction is the essence. Their volumes vary with the starting material. The liquid that stays in the chamber is the serum, also called the retentate.
