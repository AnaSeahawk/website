A Website of One’s Own

Begun together, then handed over

A website can be a home: a place where a life and its work are gathered, arranged and visited. Like any home, it is happiest when the one it belongs to lives in it and tends it.

The work here is a beginning made together. A site is planned and built alongside its owner, in their own words, images and colours. Then the keys pass over: how a page is changed, how a change goes live, how nothing is ever lost. Support stays close for as long as it is wanted.

Some want only the first spark and the map. Some want a hand on the whole of it for a season. The shape follows how much each person wishes to take part.

How it goes

From a first page to a site that is tended

A first conversation. What the site is for, who it welcomes, and how it should look and feel. The work, and its cost, are shaped here, since every site asks for something different.

A first page. Often a single page holding the essentials: who, what is offered, and how to reach out. Real words and real photographs from the start, so the site is recognisable early.

An address of its own. A domain name that belongs to the owner, so the site can be found and kept under their name.

Growing it together. Pages arrive as the work asks for them: services, writing, a gallery, a booking link. Each change is looked at, talked over and refined.

Learning to tend it. How to ask Claude for a change and read what it did, how to see a change before it is published, how to step back to an earlier version. As much or as little as is wanted.

Support afterwards. Access can stay shared, so a hand is nearby to fix something, add something, or simply take it on when tending feels like too much.

The craft

Plain pages, kept on GitHub

The sites are made of plain pages. Plain pages load quickly, last for years and carry no monthly platform fee, no template boxing in the design and no plugins waiting to be updated.

They live on GitHub, a place made for keeping work as it changes. Every version is saved, so a mistake can always be undone. Two people can tend the same site, each seeing what the other changed. GitHub Pages publishes the site at no cost, and the whole of it belongs to its owner, ready to move wherever they choose.

How it is made

Claude at the bench

The sites are built with Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, and learning to work with it is part of what is taught. Another assistant, such as ChatGPT, can serve too; Claude is the one used here.

Claude carries the technical labour that once took years to learn: writing the code behind each page, fitting it to phones and wide screens, checking links, explaining each step in plain words, and publishing when asked. The vision, the words, the images and every decision stay with the person whose site it is. Nothing changes unless it is asked for, and nothing goes live without their say.

The work does not pass to the AI. Words, photographs and pages are kept in a GitHub account under the owner’s name, or held in shared care until they are ready to hold it themselves. Anthropic’s terms assign to the user whatever rights it might have in what Claude writes. And because GitHub records every change, each one can be seen, compared and undone: the history of the site is its owner’s record.

Anyone who has tried to build and keep a site alone knows how the learning can swallow the making. With Claude, the hours that went to code go back to the work itself. A tool like this takes no one’s craft away. It puts a craft within reach of people who could not have reached it alone, and leaves more of life for creating.
