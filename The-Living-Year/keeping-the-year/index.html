The calendar names it. The sky locates it.

A date at the top of a page usually says only what the calendar says. Calendars change; they have been reformed, renamed and moved by whole days. The Sun and the Moon keep their own count. Where the Sun stands in its year, how far the Moon has travelled from it, and the place on Earth where the page was written: together they mark a day that any later reader can find again in the sky.

Keeping the Year is a Circle for learning to write that line. With the Soli Lunar Guide in hand, each person finds the Sun’s degree, the Moon’s phase and the year, and dates a first page by them.

What the Circle holds

  • ☉
    The year as a circle and a wave

    Three hundred and sixty degrees from the first of Aries. The four gates where the seasons turn, and the Sun’s climb and descent drawn as a wave.

  • ☽
    The Moon in relation to the Sun

    The phases as a distance: the new Moon when the two stand together, the full Moon when they stand opposite, and the lunation as a second ring inside the year.

  • ⌖
    Where the day stands

    Today’s Sun and Moon, found together. Solar noon at each person’s own place, set beside the noon of the clock.

  • ✦
    A year of the world

    Anno Mundi, the year counted from the Sun’s entry into Aries, and how it sits beside the common year.

  • ✎
    The first entry

    Everyone dates and writes one page in their own notebook, there in the Circle.

At each season’s gate

The Circle opens every season, at the gate where the Sun enters its sign. The spring Circle falls in the seventeen days before the year’s first new moon, so the first lunation is written from its start.

Spring20 March 2027The Sun enters Aries. The Living Year begins.
Summer21 June 2027The Sun enters Cancer, highest.
Autumn23 September 2027The Sun enters Libra.
Winter22 December 2027The Sun enters Capricorn, lowest.

Each one is complete on its own. Coming once is enough to keep a notebook for the whole year; returning at the next gate brings the season’s observations into the Circle.

Taking part

Any notebook will do: blank, lined, small enough to carry. Bring a pencil, and the Soli Lunar Guide, printed or on a screen. It is free, and arrives by email. No knowledge of astronomy or astrology is needed; the guide does the counting.

The Circle closes with open conversation: a time to share what the sky and the season are bringing, as much as to ask questions. The conversation moves around the whole circle, among everyone there.

The sky is our timekeeper.

Joining

The first Keeping the Year opens The Living Year at the spring gate, between 20 March and the new moon of 6 April 2027. The Living Waters follows in the year’s first lunation. Dates, seats and costs are listed at least one week ahead, here and in the new-moon letter, and seats are booked one at a time.

To take the subject alone, book a 90-minute conversation and name Keeping the Year. For a Circle of your own group, write to Ana.
