The Living Year

An invitation to inhabit time.

The Living Year is a gathering place for exploring the rhythms of the Sun and Moon, the living arts, and the relationships that unfold through conversation, observation and practice.

The Sun sets the year and its four seasons. The Moon sets the months. Through Circles and the Soli Lunar Guide, the turning year is followed together, along with the processes taking shape in each life, with curiosity and attention.

The year as a wave

Seen from Earth, the Sun climbs north through spring, stands highest at the Cancer gate, crosses back at Libra and stands lowest at Capricorn. Drawn across the year, that climb and descent is a wave.

Each dot is a new moon, placed where the Sun stands that day. From Aries 2027 to Aries 2028, three new moons fall inside each season.

The year turns again when the Sun enters Aries on 20 March 2028, at 02:17. Dates and times are in Universal Time. Ana travels, and the Circles come from wherever the year finds her, so no single place sets the clock. Each Circle is listed in Universal Time and in the reader’s own time.

Circles

A Circle is a gathering around one subject. Even when Ana presents, every voice counts, and the meeting between the people in the room is the point, more than the questions put to the host.

Some Circles are free and some are paid. Each listing says which, what it costs, and how many seats it holds, and seats are booked one at a time. Every Circle is announced at least one week ahead. Any Circle can also be taken alone, as a conversation.

Keeping the Year

Keeping the Year grew out of Journal Like the Alchemists. It is the Circle that opens each season: learning to keep a notebook the way the old alchemists kept theirs, by the Sun’s year and the cycles of the Moon, with the Soli Lunar Guide in hand. Complete on its own, and the natural way into the rest of the year.

The calendar names it. The sky locates it.

The Soli Lunar Guide

A short guide to dating any notebook by the sky: where the Sun stands in its year, the phase of the Moon, and the place on Earth where the page was written. The line at the top of this page is written the same way. The guide is free, and arrives this winter. After it comes a short letter at each new moon, with what the lunation brings: new writing, observations, and the Circles ahead.
