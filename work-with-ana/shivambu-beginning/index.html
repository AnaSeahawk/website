Shivambu:Beginning

A conversation about the body’s own waters, and the questions that bring us to them.

THE WORKSHOP

Live by video.
One to one: 90 minutes.
Groups: two hours, 90 minutes of exploration followed by 30 minutes of open discussion.

Perhaps someone has mentioned Shivambu and you have never quite forgotten it. Perhaps you have questions you would rather ask in a conversation than type into a search engine. Or perhaps you have already begun and would welcome some company as you find your way.

Shivambu is a name for the practice of working with one’s own urine. It is a subject that can stir curiosity, discomfort, fascination, and very practical questions—sometimes all at once. This workshop offers room to explore those responses and get to know the practice, with someone for whom it has become part of everyday life.

Drawing on daily practice since April 2022 and the reading and research that have accompanied it, I share my experience and make space for yours. It’s for anyone considering a beginning, already taking first steps, or simply wanting to understand.

Menstruation is part of the conversation too: how practitioners approach their moon cycle, and the questions that arise as the body’s rhythms change. The discussion can be shaped around the interests and circumstances of a particular group.

What we’ll explore

We begin with the name and the traditions associated with it, then move into the questions that make the subject personal and practical.

  • ≋
    The name, the history, and the reading

    An introduction to Shivambu and its traditional context, alongside books such as John Armstrong’s The Water of Life and Martha Christy’s Your Own Perfect Medicine. We make room to distinguish traditional teachings, personal accounts, and research.

  • ≋
    Approaching a beginning

    Different ways people encounter the practice, externally and internally, and the questions surrounding collection, timing, taste, and food. There is space to consider your own comfort, circumstances, and boundaries.

  • ≋
    Noticing the body’s waters

    The kidneys’ role in producing urine, what practitioners notice from day to day, and how to keep a simple record of observations without rushing to explain every change.

  • ≋
    Finding room in everyday life

    Privacy, living with other people, and deciding when—or whether—to share a personal practice with those around you.

  • ≋
    The moon cycle

    Menstruation as part of the subject from the beginning, with room for different experiences and questions.

Throughout, there is time to ask what a term means, return to something unfamiliar, or bring forward a question of your own. The group workshop closes with a further half hour of open discussion.

Taking part

No experience is needed, and nothing needs to be collected or prepared. Bring a notebook, something to write with, drinking water, and whatever questions have brought you here.

Listening is enough. Attending does not require a decision to practise or a willingness to share intimate details. The intention is to leave with a clearer understanding of the subject and more room to consider what it means to you.

Room for every question.
